--- Slide 4 ---
Introduction
Two main types of opinions
  Regular opinions: Sentiment/opinion expressions on some target entities.
    Direct opinions: “The touch screen is really cool”
    Indirect opinions: “After taking the drug, my pain has gone”
  Comparative opinions: Comparisons of more than one entity.
    E.g., “iPhone is better than Blackberry”
  We focus on regular opinions first, and just call them opinions.

--- Slide 5 ---
A Model of opinion mining
An opinion is a quadruple (Liu, 2012)
  (target, sentiment, holder, time)
  Concise but not easy to use!!!
    E.g. “The voice quality of iPhone is amazing.” (target = voice quality??? Not quite.)
An opinion is a quintuple
  (entity, aspect, sentiment, holder, time)
    Entity: target entity (or object).
    Aspect: feature of the entity.
    Sentiment: positive, negative, neutral, a rating or an emotion,
    Holder: opinion holder.
    Time: time when the opinion was expressed.

--- Slide 8 ---
Opinion Summary
With a lot of opinions, a summary is necessary.
  A multi-document summarization task.
For factual texts, summarization is to select the most important facts and present them in a sensible order while avoiding repetition.
  1 fact = any number of the same fact
But for opinion documents, it is different because opinions have a quantitative side & have targets
  1 opinion ≠ a number of opinions
Aspect-based summary is more suitable.

--- Slide 12 ---
Reason for Opinion
A reason is the justification or explanation of the opinion.
There are two main cases:
  Negative about an entity due to a bad aspect.
    I hate this car as it eats to much gas.
  Negative about an aspect because of a reason.
    This car is too small.

--- Slide 13 ---
Qualifier of Opinion
A qualifier of an opinion limits or modifies the meaning of the opinion.
It tells what an opinion is useful for, e.g.
  This car is too small for a tall person.
  The picture quality of night shots is bad.
Not every opinion comes with an explicit reason and/or qualifier.
  This car is bad.

--- Slide 17 ---
Sentiment classification
Lexicon - based approach (Taboada et al., 2011 )
  Using a set of sentiment terms, called the sentiment lexicon.
    Positive words: great, beautiful, amazing, …
    Negative words: bad, unreliable, terrible awful, …
  The score opinion value for each sentiment term is assigned a value from [-5, +5].
  Decide the sentiment of a review by aggregating scores from all sentiment terms.

--- Slide 27 ---
Aspect extraction
Using supervised learning
  Using sequence labelling methods such as Hidden Markov Models, Conditional Random Fields …
  Once aspect expressions are discovered, group them into aspect categories.
    E.g. power usage, battery life.
    Based on some similarity methods but it needs a taxonomy of aspects.
      Mapping: the system maps each discovered aspect to an aspect node in the taxonomy.
      Similarity metrics: string similarity, synonym and other distance measures using WordNet.

--- Slide 30 ---
Aspect extraction
Topic modelling
  A topic is basically an aspect.
    A document is a distribution over topics.
    A topic is a distribution over words (price, cost, cheap, expensive …).
  Aspect extraction has two tasks
    Extract aspect expressions
    Cluster them (same: picture, photo, image)
  Top models such as: pLSA (Hofmann, 1999 ) and LDA (Blei et al, 2003).

--- Slide 32 ---
Sentiment (opinion) lexicon
List of words or expressions used to express people’s subjective feeling and sentiments/opinions.
They are instrumental for sentiment analysis.
There seem to be endless variety of sentiment bearing expression.
Many of them are context dependent, not just application domain dependent.
Three main ways to compile such list:
  Manual approach
  Corpus-based approach
  Dictionary-based approach.
