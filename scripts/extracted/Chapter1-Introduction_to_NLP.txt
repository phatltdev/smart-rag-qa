Sentence splitting
  The process of separating free-flowing text into sentences.
  It is one of the first steps in any natural language processing (NLP) application.
  A period (.) may indicate the end of a sentence
    or may denote an abbreviation, a decimal point, an ellipsis, or an email address, among other possibilities.
  Question marks and exclamation marks can be similarly ambiguous due to use in emoticons, computer code, and slang.

Tokenization
  What is a word?
    “It’s”, “don’t”, “data-mining”, “trường”, “trường học”
  Divide into units to process and call it a “Token”.

--- Slide 13 ---
NLP tasks
VnCoreNLP: A Fast and Accurate Vietnamese Word Segmenter.
  The method starts with a manually word segmented sentences (e.g. “thuế_thu_nhập cá_nhân”) and its BI-formed representation (e.g. “thuế/B thu/I nhập/I cá/B nhân/I”).
  Then the syllables are extracted to construct the raw corpus (“thuế thu nhập cá nhân”).
  The initial segmenter (the longest matching strategy) is applied on the input raw corpus.
    “thuế thu nhập cá nhân”  “thuế/B thu/B nhập/I cá/B nhân/I.”
  The BI-formed gold standard corpus and the BI-formed initialized corpus are compared to generate a 5-syllable context dictionary D where each key-value pair consists of a 5-syllable window tuple as key and a gold standard tag as value.
  Based on the 5-syllable context dictionary D, the rule selector selects the most suitable rules to construct the SCRDR (Single Classification Ripple Down Rules) tree.

POS tagging
  The process of assigning one of the parts of speech to the given word.
    In simple words, we can say that POS tagging is a task of labelling each word in a sentence with its appropriate part of speech.
    Parts of speech include nouns, verb, adverbs, adjectives, pronouns, conjunction and their sub-categories.
  Most of the POS tagging falls under Rule Base POS tagging, Stochastic POS tagging, Hidden Markov Model (HMM) POS Tagging.
INTRODUCTION TO NLP

--- Slide 16 ---
NLP tasks
POS tagging
  Rule-based POS Tagging
    Words are assigned POS tags based on their characteristics and the context in which they appear.
    Rule-based POS taggers can be relatively simple to implement and are often used as a starting point for more complex machine learning-based taggers.
    For example:
      If the word ends in “-tion,” assign the tag “noun.”
      If the word ends in “-ment,” assign the tag “noun.”
      If the word is all uppercase, assign the tag “proper noun.”
      …

POS tagging
  Stochastic POS Tagging
    Word Frequency Approach
      based on the probability that a word occurs with a particular tag.
      it may yield inadmissible sequence of tags.
    Tag Sequence Probabilities
      It is also called n-gram approach.
      The best tag for a given word is determined by the probability at which it occurs with the n previous tags.

--- Slide 18 ---
NLP tasks
POS tagging
  Hidden Markov Model
    Consists of a set of states, each corresponding to a possible POS tag, and a set of transitions between the states.
    The model is trained on the training data to learn the probabilities of transitioning from one state to another and the probabilities of observing different words given a particular state.
      tag transition probabilities: P(ti|ti−1)
      word likelihood probabilities: P(wi|ti)

Named Entity Recognition
  Its functioning can be distilled into several key steps:
    Text Preprocessing: This typically includes tasks like tokenization (breaking the text into words or phrases) and part-of-speech tagging.
    Entity Identification: NER algorithms scan it to identify sequences of words that correspond to entities.
    Entity Classification: NER categorizes the recognized entities into predefined classes or types. Common categories include Person, Organization, Location, Date, and more.
    Contextual Analysis: NER considers the context in which these entities appear, ensuring accurate classification.

Information Extraction
  Initial processing
    The first step is to break down a text into fragments such as zones, phrases, segments, and tokens.
    In the initial processing stage, part-of-speech tagging, and phrasal unit identification (noun or verb phrases) are usually the next tasks.
  Proper names identification
    One of the most important stages is the identification of various classes of proper names, such as names of people or organizations, dates, monetary amounts, places, addresses, and so on.
    Regular expressions, which are a collection of patterns, are used to recognize these names.

  An approach to natural language processing that identifies the emotional tone behind a body of text.
  A popular way for organizations to determine and categorize opinions about a product, service or idea.
  Sentiment analysis can extract the polarity or the amount of positivity and negativity, subject and opinion holder within the text.

--- Slide 51 ---
NLP applications
Sentiment analysis (Opinion mining)
  General steps
    Collect data.
    Clean the data. The data is processed and cleaned to remove noise and parts of speech that don't have meaning relevant to the sentiment of the text.
    Extract features. A machine learning algorithm automatically extracts text features to identify negative or positive sentiment.
    Pick an ML model. A sentiment analysis tool scores the text using a rule-based, automatic or hybrid ML model.
    Sentiment classification. Once a model is picked and used to analyse a piece of text, it assigns a sentiment score to the text including positive, negative or neutral.

--- Slide 53 ---
NLP applications
Sentiment analysis (Opinion mining)
  Fine-grained sentiment analysis breaks down sentiment indicators into more precise categories, such as very positive and very negative.
  Emotion detection analysis identifies emotions rather than positivity and negativity. Examples include happiness, frustration, shock, anger and sadness.
  Intent-based analysis recognizes motivations behind a text in addition to opinion. For example, an online comment expressing frustration about changing a battery may carry the intent of getting customer service to reach out to resolve the issue.
  Aspect-based analysis examines the specific component being positively or negatively mentioned. For example, a customer might review a product saying the battery life was too short.
